### 📖 Introduction
The oscilloscope is a fundamental instrument in electronics and telecommunications. Unlike a multimeter, which only shows static voltage values, an oscilloscope allows visualization of **time-varying signals**, which is crucial for analyzing AC waveforms, digital pulses, and complex signals in communications systems.  

In telecommunications, oscilloscopes are used to:  
- Observe waveform shapes of signals to ensure proper signal transmission.  
- Measure amplitude and voltage swings.  
- Analyze frequency content and timing characteristics.  
- Troubleshoot circuits and communication systems.

**Theoretical Background:**  
1. **Amplitude (V)** – The maximum voltage reached by a waveform.  
2. **Peak-to-Peak Voltage (Vpp)** – Difference between the waveform’s maximum and minimum values.  
3. **Period (T)** – The time it takes for a waveform to complete one full cycle.  
4. **Frequency (f)** – Number of cycles per second, calculated as:  
\[
f = \frac{1}{T}
\]  
5. **Coupling (AC/DC)** – Determines whether DC or AC components of the signal are observed.  
6. **Triggering** – Ensures the waveform is stable on the display by starting the sweep at a defined voltage level.

Mastering these concepts ensures accurate analysis of communication signals, which is critical for analog and digital communication systems.

---

### 🧰 Materials
- Dual-channel oscilloscope (~20 MHz or higher)  
- Function generator or CAL signal source  
- Oscilloscope probe  
- Connecting wires and power supply  
- Calculator and notebook  

---

### 🔬 Procedure
1. Power on the oscilloscope and allow it to stabilize.  
2. Connect the probe to the signal source (e.g., function generator or CAL output).  
3. Adjust **vertical controls**: set input coupling to AC, vertical scale (V/div), and position for proper display.  
4. Adjust **horizontal controls**: time/div and horizontal position to clearly show at least one full cycle.  
5. Set **trigger controls**: sweep mode to AUTO, source to CH1, slope to positive, and level to mid-point.  
6. Observe the waveform and ensure it is stable.  
7. Measure the **peak-to-peak voltage (Vpp)** using vertical divisions:  
\[
Vpp = \text{vertical divisions} \times \text{V/div setting}
\]  
8. Measure the **period (T)** using horizontal divisions:  
\[
T = \text{horizontal divisions} \times \text{s/div setting}
\]  
9. Calculate the **frequency (f)**:  
\[
f = \frac{1}{T}
\]  
10. Record all results carefully.

### 📊 Observations

- Amplitude and period matched theoretical expectations for the CAL signal.  
- Oscilloscope allowed visualization of both AC and DC components depending on the coupling setting.
